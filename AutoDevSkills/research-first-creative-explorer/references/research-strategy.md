# Research strategy (Phases A–C)

Research is the fuel for non-obvious ideas. Skipping it produces clichés drawn
from the model's crowded center. Do the research first.

## Phase A — Frame the question

Answer before researching:

- **Intent type:** opportunity-finding, concept-invention, or problem-solving?
  (These need different research emphases.)
- **Real constraints vs assumed constraints.** Write both lists. Assumed
  constraints ("it must be an app", "it must make money from users", "it must use
  our stack") are candidates to drop in Phase D.
- **Drop-the-form test:** what could this become if the currently-assumed solution
  form (app, website, subscription…) were forbidden?
- **Ask vs assume:** only ask the user when a missing fact would materially change
  the *research direction* (e.g. region, target person, hard budget/skill limit).
  Otherwise state a reasonable assumption explicitly and proceed. Do not stall a
  creative task on small choices.

## Phase B — Research the world

### Build a diverse query set (VI + EN)

Run queries in **both Vietnamese and English** — they surface different sources,
markets, and workarounds. Cover these archetypes (not just one):

- **Needs / behavior / complaints:** what people struggle with, rant about, hack
  around ("tại sao ... khó chịu", "why is ... so frustrating", forums, reviews).
- **Current & failed solutions:** what exists, what shut down and *why* (failure
  post-mortems are gold — they reveal real constraints, not imagined ones).
- **Adjacent industries with the same problem structure:** who else solved this
  *shape* of problem in a totally different field?
- **Shifts:** technological, social, legal/regulatory, demographic, cultural,
  economic. New shifts open opportunity windows before the crowd arrives.
- **Workarounds:** what are people already doing manually / with spreadsheets /
  with duct-tape? A strong workaround signals an unmet, validated need.
- **Unnamed needs:** things people do but have no word for yet.
- **Edge cases as future markets:** the weird 2% who use something in an
  unintended way often preview a mainstream behavior.

**Avoid** "best idea/app for X" and "top startups in X" style queries — they
return the homogenized center and re-anchor you. Prefer queries about *behavior,
friction, and change*, not about *products*.

### Source discipline

- Prefer **primary and official sources**, original research, first-hand accounts.
- Use reliable sources for any factual claim. Cite the source **inline, next to
  the claim it supports** — not in a dump at the end.
- Label every finding as one of:
  - **Fact** — directly supported by a cited source.
  - **Inference** — a reasonable deduction from the evidence (say from what).
  - **Speculation** — a creative hypothesis to be tested (flag it as such).
- **Never fabricate** data, market sizes, user needs, quotes, citations, or
  confidence levels. "I don't have data on X" is a valid, required answer. If a
  concept rests on an unverified assumption, name it as the concept's key risk
  rather than inventing evidence for it.

### When you have no research access (offline runner)

The AutoDev runner may execute without web access. If you cannot reach sources:

- **Say so explicitly** at the top of the findings — do not pretend you researched.
- Proceed using domain knowledge, but label every claim **Inference** or
  **Speculation**, never **Fact**. Reserve **Fact** for something you actually
  verified with a source.
- Make "no external validation was possible" the concept's **top risk**, and make
  the cheapest experiment a research/interview step.
- **Never** substitute invented numbers, market sizes, or citations for the
  research you couldn't do. Absence of data is a finding, not a gap to paper over.

### Keep the report out of the skill

Research improves the *design of the ideas*; do not dump a long research report as
the deliverable. Surface only the findings that change the idea space (Phase B →
output item 2), tagged and cited.

## Phase C — Map the opportunity space

Before writing concrete ideas, sketch a multi-axis map so ideas spread across the
space instead of clustering. Pick the axes that fit the task; do not treat this
list as fixed:

- user / segment (incl. tiny, intense-need niches)
- context & environment
- underlying need
- emotion / felt experience
- moment / trigger in time
- current behavior & alternative
- untapped resource (data, place, community, waste, downtime, physical object)
- medium / technology (software is only one)
- interaction mechanism
- distribution model
- value / capture model
- degree of strangeness
- time horizon: now · ~2 years · ~5 years+

The map's job is coverage: after Phase D, check that ideas landed in *different*
regions of the map, not one corner.
